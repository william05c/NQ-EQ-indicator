MEGA PROMPT — first-two-hour cash-window study
===============================================

ROLE
Act as three people at once, and label which one is speaking when it matters:
  (a) QUANT RESEARCHER  — formulate hypotheses, test them, report what survives.
  (b) RISK MANAGER      — size the thing, measure the tail, state the ruin path.
  (c) QUANT ENGINEER    — make it reproducible: committed code, committed data
                          provenance, one command to rebuild every number.

CONTEXT ALREADY ESTABLISHED IN THIS REPO (do not re-derive, do not contradict)
  - Daily model: predicts a session's high/low band from prior daily bars.
    Measured by MAE and coverage. It is NOT a strategy and has no win rate.
  - Intraday nowcast: P(new high) / P(new low) from the running extremes.
    Calibrated on 765 sessions, worst decile error 2.6 points. NOT a strategy.
  - A TRADING RULE is a third object: entry at a band touch, exit by
    target/stop/close. Every win rate in this repo belongs to a rule.
  - Data: CME NQ 1-minute, 2022-12-26 → 2025-12-11, 765 usable sessions.
    The daily band source is a BACK-ADJUSTED continuous series and sits up to
    24% away from raw intraday prices — bands MUST be rescaled per session by
    intradayOpen/dailyOpen before use. Skipping this invalidated two earlier
    results and is the single largest source of error in this project's history.
  - H = half the band width = (upper band − lower band)/2. All targets and stops
    are quoted in H units so sessions of different volatility are comparable.
  - Win = net P&L per trade > 0 after a 2 bp round-trip cost.
  - Expectancy is the decision metric. Win rate without expectancy is decoration.

TASK
Study ONE window only: entries restricted to 09:30–11:30 ET (elapsed 930–1050
minutes after the 18:00 session open). Report everything below.

1. STRATEGY SPECIFICATION, EXACT AND REPLICABLE
   For each variant state: direction, entry trigger, entry price rule, target,
   stop, TIME STOP, and what happens if nothing triggers. Anything ambiguous is a
   bug — the reader must be able to rebuild the trade list without asking.

2. THE GRID
   direction × target f ∈ {0.1,0.2,0.3,0.5,0.8,1.0} × stop g ∈ {0.3,0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0,3.0}
   with a time stop at 11:30 ET. Report n, win rate, expectancy, total, t-stat,
   avg win, avg loss, stop count, time-stop count.

3. PER-TRADE LOG for the best few variants: timestamp, side, entry, exit, why,
   P&L. Committed to the repo so the trades can be replayed bar by bar.

4. RISK ASSESSMENT
   - Distribution of per-trade P&L: mean, sd, skew, kurtosis, p5/p50/p95, worst.
   - VaR95 and CVaR95 per trade.
   - Maximum drawdown, and time under water.
   - Bootstrap CI (10,000 resamples) on expectancy and on win rate.
   - Ruin analysis: at what position size does a 20% drawdown become likely?
   - Kelly fraction implied by the measured edge, and why it is almost certainly
     the wrong number to use when the edge is not statistically distinguishable
     from zero.

5. MULTIPLE TESTING
   State how many cells were tested and compute the Bonferroni threshold. At 144
   cells, a |t| of 2 is expected by chance about 6.6 times — say so explicitly.

6. ROBUSTNESS
   - Split by calendar year (2023, 2024, 2025) and check sign stability.
   - Parameter stability: does the sign of expectancy flip for small changes in
     f and g? A strategy whose sign flips under a 0.1H change is noise.
   - Sensitivity to the entry rule: first touch vs requiring the break to persist.

7. HONEST CONCLUSION
   State plainly whether any variant is worth trading. If none is, say that in the
   first sentence of the section, and give the number that kills it.

OUTPUT
  - src/first2h_study.js               the study, one command, prints its table
  - reports/first2h_results.json       machine-readable results + trade log
  - reports/first2h_report.html        self-contained dashboard, offline, no CDN
  - docs/backtesting/first2h.md        the written specification + findings
  - commit and push everything

STYLE
Numbers, not adjectives. Every figure reproducible from committed code and data.
If a claim fails, publish the failure as prominently as a success. Never report a
win rate without the expectancy and the stop count beside it.
